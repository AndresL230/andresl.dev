# Design Context — andresl.dev

This file is the source of truth for design decisions on this site. Read it before changing visual treatment, copy tone, or adding new sections.

## Users

A mixed audience that all share one trait: technically literate people Andres might want to work with or for. In practice that's:

- Recruiters and engineering managers at devtools / edge / infra companies
- Founders looking at interns or early hires
- Peer engineers and other students
- Hackathon judges, grant reviewers, fellowship people
- Designers / design-minded engineers

The job-to-be-done is the same for all of them: figure out, in under a minute, whether Andres ships work worth their time. Secondary: figure out *what kind* of engineer he is (the answer the site wants to deliver: a builder with taste, who likes infrastructure and the developer-experience layer above it).

Reading context: usually a laptop, in a tab next to a resume PDF or a LinkedIn profile. Occasionally mobile. Almost never in a dark room — the warm light theme is correct for this.

## Brand Personality

Three concrete words: **crafted, terminal, unhurried.**

- **Crafted** — paper-warm background, grain overlay, hairline dividers, single accent used as punctuation. Nothing is decorative-for-decoration's-sake.
- **Terminal** — `~/andresl.dev` wordmark, mono section labels (`01 — about`, `/ the work`), padded indices (`01 / 02`), JetBrains Mono carries all UI chrome.
- **Unhurried** — `0.85s` reveal durations with `cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)`, large generous type, no parallax tricks, no scroll-jacking, no "tap anywhere to start." The site assumes the visitor is intelligent and gives them room.

Voice in copy is dry and slightly self-aware: *"Something about a 36-hour clock and three strangers in a basement out of coffee brings out the best work."* / *"the occasional argument about whether type systems are worth it (they are)."* Confident, not boastful. Specific, not corporate.

Emotional goal in the first five seconds: **"this person has taste *and* ships."** Quiet confidence. The orange dot after "Andres Lopez**.**" is the entire thesis in one punctuation mark — a maker's signature.

## Aesthetic Direction

Editorial-meets-terminal on warm paper. Think *Field Notes* notebook crossed with a 1980s mainframe manual, printed on cream stock, with one ink color.

**Theme** — light, warm. `--color-bg: #f5efe1` (tan-leaning off-white), `--color-text: #1c1814` (warm ink, not black). Theme is non-negotiable; this is daytime portfolio reading, not a trading dashboard.

**Color** — strict monochromatic warm palette with a single accent:
- `--color-accent: #dd5a22` — orange. Used as: punctuation dot after proper nouns, section index numerals, in-build indicator, selection highlight, focus ring, primary CTA fill. **Rare and load-bearing.** Do not splash it.
- Neutrals are tinted warm (the surface shifts are all warm-stone variants of the cream).
- No second accent color. No gradients. No dark mode. No pure black or pure white.

**Type pairing** —
- Display: **Geist** (medium / 500), tight tracking (`-0.025em` to `-0.045em` on the largest sizes), `text-balance`, near-1.0 line-height on heroes.
- UI / labels / meta: **JetBrains Mono** at `0.66rem–0.78rem`, wide tracking (`0.14em–0.22em`), `uppercase`. Used for: nav, section headers, badges, tech tags, button labels, micro-copy.
- Body prose: Geist at body size with relaxed `1.7–1.8` line-height in `--color-text-dim`.

Mono is allowed here despite the general rule against "mono as shorthand for technical" — because this is a developer's personal site and the mono is doing structural work (labels, indices, terminal references), not vibes work. Don't loosen this and start using mono for body prose.

**Layout** —
- Centered max-width `1240px` with `px-6 md:px-10` gutters.
- Sections are vertically generous: `py-28 md:py-44`, separated by single `border-t border-line` hairlines.
- 12-col grids for body sections, with mono labels in `md:col-span-3` and prose in `md:col-span-9` — a magazine sidebar pattern.
- Asymmetry over centered slabs. Left-aligned content with right whitespace.
- No card grids. Project entries are full-bleed image + headline, not stamped-out cards.

**Texture** —
- SVG grain overlay (`.grain`) at `0.07` opacity with `mix-blend-mode: multiply`. Always on. This is the single biggest reason the site doesn't read as AI-generated.
- Hairline borders (`rgba(28, 24, 18, 0.10)`) for separation, never heavy strokes.
- Soft, low shadow only where elevation is meaningful (rare).

**Motion** —
- One easing curve: `cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)` (exponential ease-out). No bounce, no elastic.
- Durations cluster at `0.6s / 0.7s / 0.85s` — slow by app standards, correct for a portfolio.
- `framer-motion` `whileInView` with `viewport={{ once: true, margin: '-80px' }}` is the section reveal pattern. Don't re-animate on re-entry.
- Hero has a canvas particle field that reacts to the cursor (`scatter` variant). It's the site's one piece of "showing off" — keep it subtle, keep it cream/ink, never let it dominate.
- Scroll progress bar (`h-px bg-accent/80`) at the bottom of the nav.
- `.breathe` animation on the "in build" indicator dot.

**Anti-references** — what this site is explicitly *not*:
- Not Vercel-clone dark navy with glowing accents.
- Not Linear-clone glassmorphism with backdrop-blur stacks.
- Not the Brittany Chiang green-on-navy `brittanychiang.com` template that every CS student copies.
- Not Notion/Stripe-style pastel rounded SaaS marketing.
- Not Awwwards-style scroll-jacked WebGL parallax.
- Not "developer terminal" in the lazy sense (no fake prompts, no blinking cursors as decoration, no `>` chevrons everywhere).

## Design Principles

These are the rules the site is already executing on. Future changes should reinforce them, not dilute them.

1. **The orange dot is the brand.** Every proper noun the site wants you to remember gets one: "Andres Lopez.", "Recost.", "Sapling.". Don't add a second accent color. Don't make the dot a different shape. Don't make it ironic. It's a signature; treat it like one.

2. **Mono labels, display content.** UI chrome (nav, section headers, badges, button text) is small mono with wide tracking and uppercase. Actual content (headlines, body prose) is Geist. Never mix this up — mono headlines or display labels both break the system instantly.

3. **One accent, used as punctuation.** The orange is for *moments*: the dot, the index numeral, the in-build pulse, the focus ring. If you find yourself reaching for it to color a card background or a heading, stop and pick a neutral instead.

4. **Hairlines, not boxes.** Sections separate with a 1px `border-line`. Project tiles have a single thin border. There are no drop-shadowed cards. There are no nested containers. If you want to group something, use space and a hairline.

5. **Slow on purpose.** Motion durations stay in the `0.6–0.85s` band with the exponential ease-out. The site is supposed to feel deliberate. If a reveal feels "snappy" it's wrong.

6. **The grain stays.** The SVG noise overlay is what keeps the site from reading as AI-generated. Do not remove it, do not lower the opacity below ~0.05, do not animate it.

7. **Copy is dry and specific.** No "Empowering teams to..." Write like a person describing what they actually did: *"a VS Code extension reads your code statically for cost patterns."* Names of things, sizes of things, what they do. No vibes-marketing copy.

## Notes for future Claude

- `CLAUDE.md` is out of date. It claims the project uses "plain CSS (no Tailwind, no CSS modules)" and that all project data lives in `Projects.tsx`. Reality: the project uses **Tailwind v4** (`@import "tailwindcss"` in `index.css`, `@tailwindcss/vite` plugin), framer-motion, react-router-dom v7 (multi-page), and project data lives in `src/data/projects.ts` (referenced via `@/data/projects`). When in doubt, trust the code, not `CLAUDE.md`.
- The site is single-page-feeling but actually has routes: `/`, `/work`, `/work/:slug` (with a modal-overlay pattern using `location.state.background`).
- Do not introduce a new font without going through the font-selection procedure. The current pairing (Geist + JetBrains Mono) is locked in via Google Fonts import in `index.css`.
